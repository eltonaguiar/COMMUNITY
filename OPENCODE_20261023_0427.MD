## 3. ELI5 — how the justification logic works

Nine gates, like playground checks before believing a kid's "my game always wins":

1. **Enough rounds (n≥100)** — ten lucky tosses mean nothing (our bond pick had 18 → FAIL).
2. **Wins big enough after the casino's cut (PF≥1.5)** — risk $1, net ≥$1.50 (forex had 1.44 → FAIL).
3. **Pessimistic replay still wins (PF CI-low≥1.2)** — resimulate history 4,000 ways; if bad-luck versions lose, no edge (prediction-market had CI 0.68–1.05 → FAIL).
4. **Check the account daily (MDD≤20%)** — a −80% drop counts even if you haven't sold (this killed BTC).
5. **Would random timing do as well? (MC ≥95th pct)** — 2,000 random buy dates; beat 95% of them or you're not choosing.
6. **Lucky-guess discount (DSR>0.95)** — count *every* strategy *every* agent tried (~65+); a coin flips hot eventually.
7. **Survive double fees (PF≥1.3 at 2× costs)** — thin edges die at the first cost surprise.
8. **Not one jackpot (top-symbol≤30%)** — one lucky coin ≠ a system.
9. **Worked before you knew (both halves profit)** — only-hindsight wins = curve-fitting.

**Why the index keeps passing:** it's diversified (auto-passes #8), never "times" the market (nothing to fail #5), and its only cost is the fee (#7). Every *selector* we tested broke at least one gate or showed no significant edge — hence the boring answer: cheapest broad index you can buy.
