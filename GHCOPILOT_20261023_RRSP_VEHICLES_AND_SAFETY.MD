## 1. Direct answer: trustworthy does not mean capital-safe

- **FNILX, FXAIX and FCNTX are mutual funds**, not individual-company stocks.
- **VOO, IVV, VFV, VUG, VGG, XEQT, VBAL, SHY and BND are exchange-traded funds
  (ETFs)**: pooled portfolios whose units trade on an exchange like stocks.
- Both wrappers can hold stocks, bonds or a mixture. The holdings, strategy,
  leverage and investor's currency determine much of the risk, not the wrapper.
- **Zero expense is not zero risk.** FNILX lost about 19.37% in 2022 in our
  adjusted-price data. Its measured fall from a prior peak was 33.76% in the
  comparison window. A legitimate fund can do exactly what it promises and lose.
- If "safe" means protecting principal rather than accepting market losses, an
  eligible insured deposit or Guaranteed Investment Certificate (GIC) is a more
  relevant comparison than an equity ETF. Term, early-withdrawal rights, deposit
  insurance limits and inflation still matter. We did not select a current rate.

**Explain like I am five (ELI5):** the account is the cupboard, the fund is the
basket, and stocks/bonds are what is inside. A Registered Retirement Savings Plan
(RRSP) cupboard does not stop the basket falling. A free basket does not either.

## 2. RRSP eligibility has three separate gates

1. **Legal qualification:** the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) describes which
   investments a registered plan may hold. Paragraph 1.16 of its
   [qualified-investment folio](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans/series-3-property-investments-savings-plan-folio-10-registered-plans-individuals/income-tax-folio-s3-f10-c1-qualified-investments-rrsps-resps-rrifs-rdsps-tfsas.html)
   includes ETF units listed on a designated stock exchange, subject to the
   applicable conditions. This is not a rule that every foreign mutual fund qualifies.
2. **Dealer and account access:** a legally qualifying security may still be
   unavailable at a dealer, under an account service, or for your residency.
   Currency alone does not settle this. Opening a US-dollar account is not a
   workaround for distribution/residency restrictions.
3. **Suitability and risk:** legal eligibility and the ability to place an order
   do not establish a sensible holding period, diversification, or safety.

### What the numbers mean

- **Compound annual growth rate (CAGR):** the constant annual rate that would
  connect the starting and ending values. It smooths away the bumpy ride.
- **Maximum drawdown (MDD):** largest percentage decline of the investment from
  its previous peak, using an actual compounded value path and including the
  initial value. A 33.76% decline would turn a $10,000 peak into about $6,624.
  It need not occur immediately after the initial purchase.
- **Worst rolling 252-session return:** worst measured start-to-end holding
  interval of approximately one trading year. It is not a forecast or a
  calendar-year result.
- **Annualized volatility:** standard deviation of daily percentage changes
  multiplied by the square root of 252; available in the
  [machine-readable results](GHCOPILOT_20261023_RRSP_VEHICLE_STATS.json).
  It measures variability, not a guaranteed loss limit.

No stock fund in this panel delivered guaranteed steady gains. Even VBAL fell
about 21.19% from a peak in this window. SHY's smaller US-dollar losses did not
eliminate Canadian-dollar exchange-rate risk. BND's 18.58% drawdown demonstrates
that a broad bond fund is not cash.

## 6. "Free" versus "paid": costs and small balances

There are at least four different price tags: fund operating expenses, trading
commission, bid/ask spread, and currency/account charges. Tax is another layer.
Commission-free mutual funds can still charge a meaningful annual MER.

Illustrative approximate annual embedded expense on a constant balance:

| Operating expense rate | $200 | $500 | $1,000 | $2,000 | $10,000 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| 0.03% | $0.06 | $0.15 | $0.30 | $0.60 | $3.00 |
| 0.28% | $0.56 | $1.40 | $2.80 | $5.60 | $28.00 |
| 1.94% | $3.88 | $9.70 | $19.40 | $38.80 | $194.00 |

These are scale illustrations, not additional deductions from the historical
returns already reported net of operating expenses. Fee differences do not
isolate performance causation across different mandates.

Royal Bank of Canada (RBC) Direct Investing's
[current pricing page](https://www.rbcdirectinvesting.com/pricing/index.html)
states:

- Full-Suite online stock/ETF trades ordinarily cost $9.95, with specified
  commission-free ETF exceptions.
- GoSmart has a stated 50 free stock/ETF trades per year across those accounts,
  with terms and a selected commission-free ETF list.
- Mutual funds are **commission-free in Full-Suite** and **not offered in
  GoSmart**. The separate mutual-fund page has a conflicting older fee answer;
  order-specific/current legal terms must govern.

Therefore the peer's flat $9.95 charge on every mutual-fund purchase is not a
verified current or historical tariff. At a hypothetical $9.95 fee, one $200
stock trade loses 4.975% immediately; if the actual applicable trade is free,
that loss does not exist. Do not apply one platform tariff to every product.

For affordability, a $200 three-fund basket means about $66.67 per fund, not $200
for each. A $100 fund minimum can block it. A $500 RBF460 minimum blocks a new
$200 account in that series. Whole ETF units, any permitted fractional dealing,
fund minimums and dealer access must be checked separately. No exact present
Canadian execution plan is certified by our historical US-dollar simulation.

## 7. Withholding tax: useful distinction, not a safety rating

BlackRock's
[Canadian withholding-tax guide](https://www.blackrock.com/ca/investors/en/literature/brochure/withholding-tax-reference-guide-en-ca.pdf)
explains the usual treatment:

- A US-listed ETF holding US stocks, held **directly** in a properly administered
  RRSP, generally benefits from the US dividend-withholding exemption.
- A Canadian-listed wrapper holding US stocks or a US ETF generally does not
  receive that exemption merely because the Canadian investor holds it in an RRSP.
- This is not a blanket exemption from all tax, all foreign withholding, or later
  Canadian tax on RRSP withdrawals. Other foreign markets and holdings differ.

The familiar 15% treaty withholding rate applies to applicable distributions,
**not 15% of the account value or total capital gain**. The guide is general
information, not confirmation of any person's tax residency or broker coding.
For example, at a hypothetical 1.5% dividend yield, 15% withholding is about
0.225% of fund value a year, not 15% of fund value. Conversion/spread costs can
outweigh that difference for a small short holding.

stocks/bonds and ETFs as products it does **not** insure. Eligible deposits/GICs
are a different category. Investor-protection arrangements are not guarantees
against market losses.

**Our conclusion:** low-cost diversified exposure has a defensible role.
It is not a demonstrated source of steady, high, safe gains. A trustworthy fund
is one whose mandate, custody, reporting, costs, access and risks check out—not
one promised never to lose. We cannot certify personal suitability from a ticker.
