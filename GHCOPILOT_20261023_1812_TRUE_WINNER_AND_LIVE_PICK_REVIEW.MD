## 3. My ideal evaluation protocol: technical and ELI5

### A. Establish identity, objective and honest data

**Technical:** exact instrument/share class, domicile, currency, benchmark,
decision timestamp, source and version; historical eligibility and dead/merged
instruments; corporate actions/distributions; no future data in inputs.
ETF means exchange-traded fund: a wrapper, not its own economic asset class.

**ELI5:** know what you bought, what job it is supposed to do, and what you
could really have known before choosing it.

### B. Check the economic story and fundamentals

**Technical:** understand why returns should exist and what destroys them.
For stocks: earnings, free cash flow, balance sheet, dilution and valuation.
For bonds: yield, duration, credit and refinancing. For commodities: supply,
inventory and term structure. For currency: rate differences and actual funding.
For cryptoassets: liquidity, funding, supply/unlocks and custody.
These are requirements, not claims that every input was checked today.

**ELI5:** a nice chart does not explain where the money comes from or what
could make it disappear.

### C. Reconstruct an investable account, not a pile of percentages

**Technical:** one account budget; holdings plus cash plus fees must conserve
capital. Include realistic spread, commission, currency conversion, borrow,
financing, contract multipliers, overlapping exposures and daily open-position
valuation. Fund operating expenses already embedded in published net returns
must not be deducted twice.

**ELI5:** you cannot spend the same $200 three times. Count all the money,
including losses while a position is still open.

### D. Compare like with like

**Technical:** identical dates, currency and distributions; risk/style/mandate
matched benchmarks; cash and passive alternatives; returns after implementable
costs. A balanced fund does not have to beat an all-stock index to do its job.

**ELI5:** compare bikes with bikes, not a careful bus with a racing car.

### E. Try hard to disprove the edge

**Technical:** chronologically separate training and testing; preserve every
rule tested; cluster correlated opportunities; test multiple regimes, independent
sources, removal of dominant symbols/sources and doubled trading costs.
Measure uncertainty rather than extrapolating one strong score.

For tactical promotion, our existing research screen includes at least 100 clean
resolved trades, net profit factor above 1.5 and account drawdown below 20%, with
additional uncertainty/concentration/out-of-sample checks. These are **policy
floors, not proof**, and are not blindly applied to a passive fund's buy-and-hold
role. A different return objective needs its own appropriate test.

**ELI5:** do not count ten copies of the same lucky ticket as ten discoveries,
and do not choose the best recipe after secretly trying hundreds.

### F. Freeze the rule and observe genuinely new results

**Technical:** timestamped prospective cohort; independent data/price and lifecycle
reconciliation; executable entries/exits; clear promotion and retirement rules.
No label upgrade from repeated peer citations alone.

**ELI5:** write the rules before the next game, keep every score, and let someone
else check the scoreboard.

### Metrics explained

- **Net profit factor (PF):** gains divided by the magnitude of losses after
  stated costs. PF 1.5 means $1.50 of gains per $1 of losses, not a 150% return.
- **Win rate (WR):** percentage of outcomes counted as wins. It says nothing
  alone about how large wins and losses are.
- **Sample size (n):** number of recorded observations, not necessarily
  independent opportunities.
- **Compound annual growth rate (CAGR):** smooth annual equivalent of total
  growth, not a promise of that return every year.
- **Maximum drawdown (MDD):** worst observed peak-to-trough account decline,
  including open positions and starting capital. It is not a future loss limit.
- **Confidence interval (CI):** uncertainty range under a specified model,
  not a guarantee. A large PF from four wins can have enormous uncertainty.
- **Sharpe ratio:** excess return divided by volatility under specified timing
  and annualization conventions. Mixing per-trade and daily ratios is invalid.
- **Deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR):** adjusts a Sharpe assessment for selection and
  return-distribution assumptions; not a literal probability a strategy is real.
- **Out-of-sample (OOS):** observations not used to fit/select the rule.
- **Monte Carlo (MC):** simulated comparisons under an explicitly defined null.
  A high fraction of random alternatives **beaten** is favorable; a high fraction
  **beating the strategy** is not. Fund-selection and entry-timing nulls differ.
- **Basis point:** 0.01 percentage point. **MER:** management expense ratio.
- **Alpha:** model-relative excess return, not automatically manager skill.
- **Beta:** sensitivity to a market factor, not a separate manager achievement.

**No universal “safe and strongly winning” label:** stronger growth often comes
with material drawdown risk. A 25% loss requires a 33.3% gain just to recover.
Principal-protected deposits and market investments need separate criteria.

## 12. Practical small-account requirements remain part of “good”

For $1,000/$2,000 accounts, a potentially useful fund still needs an affordable
implementation. Initial/subsequent minimum, account fees, unit price/fractional
eligibility, spread, currency and automatic contribution terms all matter.

**DRIP** means dividend reinvestment plan/program. Reinvestment recycles a
distribution; it does not create return. Whole-unit-only programs may leave a
small distribution in cash. Fractional rules are broker/security-specific and
not universally confirmed in our work.
**RRSP** means Registered Retirement Savings Plan. Eligibility, residency/dealer
availability, suitability and loss risk are different tests.

See the [access and DRIP report](GHCOPILOT_20261023_FUND_ACCESS_DRIP_AND_SMALL_ACCOUNTS.MD).
No mandatory ordinary monthly holding contribution was established in the sources
checked; an optional recurring-purchase minimum is not a compulsory bill.
