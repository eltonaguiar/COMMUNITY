# $1,000 versus $2,000: fund access, ETF unit prices, monthly costs and DRIP

**Author:** GHCopilot, an artificial intelligence assistant using Copilot Software
Development Kit in Visual Studio Code. **Date:** October 23, 2026.
Research and education only; not financial or tax advice.

## Quick answer

For a normal fully paid investment, you generally do **not** owe a mandatory
monthly contribution simply to keep holding its units. Ongoing fund expenses
still reduce the value inside the fund. A broker/account fee, automatic-purchase
agreement, leveraged position or special product may have separate requirements.

Do not confuse:

1. **Initial minimum:** minimum needed to open that exact mutual-fund series.
2. **Subsequent minimum:** smallest later purchase; not necessarily compulsory.
3. **Monthly contribution:** an optional savings plan unless its actual terms
   make contributions a condition of a promotion, fee waiver or product.
4. **ETF unit price:** cash needed for one exchange-traded fund unit if the
   dealer requires whole units. Fractional eligibility changes that barrier.
5. **Account fee / trade fee / fund expense:** three separate costs.
6. **DRIP:** dividend reinvestment plan or program, recycling distributions into
   additional units. It is not new return or a promise of profit.

ELI5: buying the basket, adding money to it, paying the shop and recycling its
cash payouts are different things.

## 2. Does the ETF's unit price matter? Yes for execution, not cheapness

An ETF at $20 is not necessarily cheaper-valued than one at $200. Unit count is
arbitrary and can change through splits. Value the underlying portfolio and fees,
not the printed unit price.

But if only whole units are available, the unit price determines how much cash
you can deploy and whether a small distribution buys another unit.

### Concrete illustration using VFV's observed price, not a current quote

Vanguard's product page retrieved in our earlier check listed VFV market price
**CAD $190.42 for October 22, 2026**. The following holds that price constant
solely for arithmetic. It is not a live executable quote.
The $9.95 case is a hypothetical applicable full-suite ETF trade fee; selected
commission-free ETFs, account allowances or different dealers can change it.
We have not certified VFV's current inclusion on a dealer's free list.

| Starting CAD | Purchase fee | Whole units | Amount in units | Cash left |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $1,000 | $0 | 5 | $952.10 | $47.90 |
| $1,000 | $9.95 | 5 | $952.10 | $37.95 |
| $2,000 | $0 | 10 | $1,904.20 | $95.80 |
| $2,000 | $9.95 | 10 | $1,904.20 | $85.85 |

Formula: whole units = floor((capital - applicable fee) / unit price).
Cash + invested value + actual charged fee must equal the starting budget.
An unaffordable attempted order is not modeled as successfully executed.

With eligible fractional dealing, more of the budget may be invested, but
fractional purchase, recurring purchase, dividend reinvestment and transfer
support are separate broker features. Do not assume that offering one means all.
Bid/ask spread, price movement and order handling still matter.

## 3. DRIP: what it does, and what a small account might miss

A DRIP reinvests a cash distribution rather than paying it out to spend.
This can reduce idle cash and simplify compounding, but it does not manufacture
a return: on the ex-distribution date the fund's value ordinarily adjusts for
the distribution, all else equal.

There are at least three implementations:

- **Mutual-fund distribution reinvestment:** commonly purchases additional
  fractional units at the applicable fund price under the fund/dealer's terms.
- **Broker whole-unit ETF DRIP:** buys as many eligible whole units as the
  distribution funds, with the remainder in cash, depending on plan terms.
- **Fractional reinvestment:** eligible brokers/securities may reinvest smaller
  amounts into fractional units. Coverage, minimums, timing, price and fees
  require verification.

**Do not assume every instrument qualifies.** Check eligibility per security,
account and dealer; opt-in/enrollment, automatic versus manual processing, any
discount/fee, residual cash and currency conversion.
Reinvestment generally uses already-held account assets; it is not normally a
new cash contribution to an RRSP, unlike adding outside money.
Reinvested distributions can still have tax consequences in non-registered accounts.
Special transactions/residency rules need separate advice.

### Why unit price matters to whole-unit reinvestment

Vanguard's product page also showed **$0.41 distribution per unit**. Treat this
as a single observed distribution amount, **not** a promise about the next
payment or a forward yield forecast.

At that distribution and the illustrative $190.42 reinvestment price:

- Five units produce **$2.05**. A whole-unit-only plan cannot buy another unit.
- Ten units produce **$4.10**. Still not enough for another unit.
- If eligible fractional reinvestment is supported, those small amounts may
  be reinvested instead; exact plan terms were not verified here.

The simple threshold for buying one unit from **one distribution alone** is:
existing units x distribution per unit >= reinvestment unit price, before any
applicable costs. Prior cash pooling is plan-specific.
Do not project that threshold indefinitely: prices and distributions change.

**Verified versus unverified:** RBC's current pricing page advertises automatic
reinvestment on **DRIP-eligible securities** without an extra service charge.
Attempts to retrieve separate detailed DRIP/help pages returned unavailable,
blocked or unrelated content. Thus we have **not certified** its fractional/
whole-unit treatment, VFV/XEQT eligibility, or an exact ticker-specific opt-in
process. The whole-unit examples above are explicitly conditional, not asserted
as RBC's confirmed implementation.

## 4. How much must be added monthly?

**For merely holding a normal fully paid fund position:** no compulsory monthly
contribution was established by the sources checked. There is no ordinary fund
"renewal" every month. You can generally retain existing units without regularly
buying more, subject to fund/dealer balance and account terms.

**For an optional automatic savings plan:** minimum recurring purchase and
frequency can differ from ordinary subsequent minimums. Verify the plan rather
than assuming RBF460's $25 minimum is a mandatory $25 monthly bill.

**For whole-unit ETF purchases:** to buy one additional unit this month, you need
its current price plus the applicable trade cost, less usable cash already in
the account. At the illustration price, $50/month would require accumulation
before buying a $190.42 whole unit, unless fractional/recurring support permits
smaller purchases.

**For a fee waiver:** determine whether there really is a current balance or
contribution condition. RBC's current account pricing says the old maintenance
fee no longer exists; do not invent a monthly deposit to waive it.

**For leveraged/margin products:** margin calls and ongoing funding are very
different from a fully paid fund. This report does not approve those products.

## 5. Small recurring purchases: commission may dominate operating expenses

If a trade really costs $9.95:

| New money used for one purchase | Commission as a percentage of that amount |
|---|---:|
| $50 | 19.90% |
| $100 | 9.95% |
| $200 | 4.975% |
| $1,000 | 0.995% |
| $2,000 | 0.4975% |

These are one-trade friction examples, not assumed charges for every fund or
broker. Under an actually applicable zero-commission arrangement, this particular
charge is zero. Operating expenses, spread and any currency conversion remain.

Approximate **annual embedded fund expense**, holding balance constant:

| Vehicle / documented expense | On $1,000 | On $2,000 |
|---|---:|---:|
| FNILX: 0% operating expense | $0 | $0 |
| FXAIX: 0.015% | $0.15 | $0.30 |
| VOO: 0.03% | $0.30 | $0.60 |
| VFV: 0.08% MER | $0.80 | $1.60 |
| XEQT: 0.19% MER | $1.90 | $3.80 |
| VBAL: 0.22% MER | $2.20 | $4.40 |
| TDB902: 0.28% MER | $2.80 | $5.60 |
| RBF460 Series A: 1.94% MER | $19.40 | $38.80 |

Currencies and investment mandates differ. These illustrate scale, not a
performance ranking or actual invoice. Fund expenses accrue inside net asset
value (NAV), so **do not subtract them again from published net fund returns**.
No operating expense means neither zero investor costs nor zero investment risk.

## 6. Improved feature scorecard for all future fund comparisons

We now require the following access fields in addition to performance/risk:

1. Exact fund/share class, domicile, trading currency and underlying currencies.
2. Initial minimum and subsequent minimum, with source/date and unknowns.
3. Required versus optional contribution frequency; maintenance/waiver terms.
4. Actual startup/sales charge, trade commission and short-term redemption fees.
5. Whole unit price and timestamp; fractional and recurring-purchase support.
6. Distribution frequency/history and tax composition where available.
7. DRIP eligibility, enrollment, fractional/whole-unit rule, residual cash,
   execution pricing/fees and any foreign-exchange handling.
8. Account balance/transfer-out/inactivity/closure fees, distinct from the fund.
9. Currency conversion, spread and account-specific tax/withholding treatment.
10. Portfolio affordability at **each** rebalance, not just the first deposit.

Status for each is **VERIFIED / ATTRIBUTED / UNKNOWN / NOT APPLICABLE**, never
an inferred success from a missing value.
No aggregate score should hide a hard access barrier.

### What this means for our stance

Both $1,000 and $2,000 can clear the documented TDB902 and RBF460 initial fund
minimums. That alone says nothing about personal suitability or which dealer
offers the series. ETFs may reduce minimums and expenses, but high unit prices,
dealing charges and incomplete reinvestment can matter at small balances.
An accessible lower-cost vehicle can be practical without being capital-safe.
DRIP is a cash-handling feature, not evidence of predictive skill.
