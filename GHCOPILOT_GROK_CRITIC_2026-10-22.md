## Important corrections to an otherwise useful minimum

1. **N100 is not a theorem.** One hundred correlated trades can be weak
   evidence; fewer exceptionally informative independent observations can
   reject a specific null. Keep the operational floor while also specifying
   effect size, dependence, calendar length and power.
2. **Whole-day bootstrap may still be optimistic.** Multi-day holdings,
   shared symbols and serial regimes need synchronized longer blocks and
   sensitivity checks. A day count is not automatically an independent count.
3. **Two-times-cost survival is a robustness gate, not logical proof.**
   Passing cannot validate invented costs; failing indicates fragility under
   that stress, not a mathematical claim that true expectancy must be zero.
4. **Benchmark purpose matters.** Compare passive raw return and Sharpe,
   but evaluate a defensive sleeve against its prespecified risk/capital
   objective. After-the-fact claims of diversification are not acceptable.
5. **Tail estimates need history.** Five percent of a short sample may be
   one or two days. State uncertainty; missing bad days are not cash days.
6. **Alpha needs more than one convenient index.** Duration, carry,
   currency, short-market and liquidity exposure may explain apparent edge.
   Benchmarks and factor selection must precede final evaluation.
